# The Year The Label Changed

What 2026 food regulation means for a non-alcoholic beverage business

From the 38th Annual Food Label Conference, June 1-2, 2026

---

# Why You Are Hearing About This

- For years, one label worked in every state.
- That is ending in 2026.
- Federal reform and state laws are arriving at the same time.
- This briefing translates the conference into plain business terms.

---

# The Whole Story In One Line

Stop trying to predict the rules.

Get organized enough to move fast when any of them lands.

---

# Two Forces Are Colliding

- Federal reform under MAHA, the "Make America Healthy Again" agenda.
- A state patchwork of separate ingredient and labeling laws.
- Both land on the same can at the same time.

---

# The Scale Of The State Wave

- 2023: 3 ingredient bills in 2 states.
- 2025 to 2026: more than 140 bills across 42 states.
- New laws on the books in 13 states.
- More than 100 ingredients targeted somewhere.
- California leads and writes the template others copy.

---

# Five Federal Changes To Track

- GRAS overhaul: the "decide it is safe yourself" path is closing.
- Synthetic dyes: six petroleum colors phasing out by end of 2026.
- Ultra-processed food: an official definition is being written.
- Front-of-package box: a Low / Medium / High nutrition rating.
- The new "Healthy" rule: vitamins no longer rescue an unhealthy product.

---

# What GRAS Means For You

- GRAS means "Generally Recognized As Safe."
- Companies could self-certify ingredients without telling FDA.
- FDA has proposed closing that path.
- Action: keep an audit-ready safety file for every ingredient you rely on.

---

# Front-Of-Package Labeling

- A new front-label box rates Saturated Fat, Sodium, and Added Sugars.
- Each gets a Low, Medium, or High mark.
- Added sugar is the most likely "High" on a sweetened drink.
- It reshapes the front panel, so plan one coordinated redesign.

---

# California First: New Ingredient Laws

- AB 418 bans BVO and Red No. 3 in any product sold in California.
- Deadline: January 1, 2027.
- AB 660 standardizes date labels by July 1, 2026.
- BVO and Red No. 3 are the near-term beverage items. Check formulas now.

---

# California: Proposition 65

- A 1986 right-to-know law. It bans nothing; it requires warnings.
- A plaintiff only has to show a listed chemical is present.
- The burden then shifts to you to prove the exposure is safe.
- Drivers are heavy metals and acrylamide carried in by ingredients.
- Defense is ongoing: testing, supplier indemnity, clear warnings.

---

# Why Beverages Are A Target

- Added sugar is the most politically charged nutrient of 2026.
- Sugar alternatives are the next labeling battleground.
- SNAP rules in 23 states block sweetened and energy drinks.
- Some wording is broad enough to catch zero-sugar lines.
- Color and preservative swaps hit drinks directly.

---

# The Upside For A Clean Line

- Zero-sugar and dye-free is where the rules are heading.
- A good front-of-package rating can be a brand asset.
- An honest "no artificial colors" claim can be a brand asset.
- The catch: non-nutritive sweeteners are the part most likely to draw a challenge.

---

# The Marketing Lesson On Claims

- A claim is safe only where science, marketing, and legal overlap.
- Courts judge the whole package, not just the words.
- A quiet supplier swap that skips claim review is the classic trap.
- "Natural," "clean," and "guilt-free" have no fixed legal meaning.
- Anything an influencer or AI tool writes is the brand's liability.

---

# Get Organized Internally

- Regulatory decides what must be true on the label.
- Quality Assurance makes it true on every run.
- Use one versioned spec with sign-off from both.
- Decide who can authorize a "can we run?" call before the line stops.

---

# What AI Can And Cannot Do

- Clean data and a clear problem come before any tool.
- Keep a human in the loop on anything with legal risk.
- Broad, meaningful impact is five-plus years out.
- Regulatory monitoring is a valid, reviewed use case.

---

# What Talking Rain Should Do

- Build one master list of ingredients and claims, mapped by function.
- Clear BVO and Red No. 3 from California products before 2027.
- Run a Prop 65 exposure check on heavy metals and acrylamide.
- Audit front-of-package and "healthy" claims; plan one redesign window.
- Route every supplier or formula change through claims review.

---

# The Takeaway

Readiness beats prediction.

One clean list of ingredients and claims is the foundation for all of it.
