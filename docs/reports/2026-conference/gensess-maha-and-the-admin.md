# gensess-maha-and-the-admin

A federal policy briefing on the MAHA movement, the FDA leadership shake-up, and the rules, deliverables, and budget fights shaping food policy through the back half of 2026.

## Summary

Jared Rothstein of AgencyIQ, with a Politico co-presenter, delivered the densest policy deck in the set: a regulatory-intelligence read of where federal food policy stands. It defines the Make America Healthy Again agenda, tracks FDA leadership instability, inventories the agency's priority deliverables and pending rules, and closes on appropriations, FDA's legislative wish list, and the political crosscurrents heading into the midterms.

## Key Points

MAHA, coalesced around RFK Jr., centers on reducing chronic diet-related disease especially in children, reducing food and agriculture corporate influence, eliminating harmful additives such as dyes and pesticides, encouraging real food over ultra-processed food, reforming dietary guidance toward saturated fat and animal protein while discouraging added sugar and refined carbs, and pursuing radical transparency through labeling, testing, and premarket review. On leadership, Marty Makary is out as FDA Commissioner amid controversies, Kyle Diamantas is in as likely acting commissioner possibly through year end, and Don Prater, a 26-year FDA veteran, is leading the Human Foods Program with comparative stability during the transition.

FDA's 2026 priority deliverables mix continuations and new initiatives. Ongoing work includes the synthetic dye phaseout, the GRAS proposed rule, the front-of-package nutrition labeling rule, updated infant formula nutrient standards, post-market chemical assessments for phthalates, propylparaben, BHA, BHT, and ADA, Closer to Zero action levels for cadmium and inorganic arsenic in baby and toddler foods, expedited natural color additive approvals, and contaminant monitoring. New initiatives include expanded allergen labeling including gluten, voluntary caffeine labeling guidance, microplastics test-method development, guidelines to limit food advertising to children, an added-sugar reduction strategy, and evaluation of Phase I sodium targets. Open points include a revived but narrower recall modernization, possible revision of the "healthy" claim, no committed UPF timeline, and an FOP scope still in flux.

On rules specifically, the UPF definition and GRAS substances rules are at the proposed stage, standards-of-identity revocations and the orange-juice modernization are final-stage, cadmium action levels for baby food are proposed, salt substitutes in standardized foods are final, and FOP is proposed-or-final. The front-of-package rule could depart substantially from the Biden-era proposal: Kennedy has connected it to UPFs and floated a green/yellow/red traffic-light scheme replacing the proposed monochromatic added-sugar/saturated-fat/sodium box, Makary questioned saturated fat as a top-three label feature, and Diamantas is weighing whether to re-propose or finalize under Administrative Procedure Act considerations, raising the prospect of a new notice of proposed rulemaking. The UPF definition RFI closed October 2025 with thousands of comments showing disagreement over the NOVA system, support for nutrient criteria, a preference for label-based definitions, requests for category carveouts, and some opposition to defining UPF at all. The GRAS proposed rule has been at OIRA since December 1, points toward scaled-down reform, and turns on whether it requires only forward-looking notification or also retrospective reporting to credibly "close the loophole."

The deck also covered color additive approvals (beetroot red and spirulina extract approved, six recent natural-color approvals, two CAPs remaining, possible tightening of impurity limits), post-market chemical activity (BHA, BHT, and ADA as next targets with RFIs issued), and infant formula testing (312 samples across 16 brands, more than 120,000 data points, mostly low contaminant levels, with Closer to Zero expansion under Operation Stork Speed). Dietary Guidelines implementation flows into military, VA, and prison procurement, school meals, WIC, and the FDA "healthy" claim. On SNAP, 23 states have approved waivers restricting soda, candy, and sweetened beverages, with a multi-state implementation table, an FNS 90-day enforcement grace period that has now ended for several states, and litigation by recipients challenging the waivers. The FY 2027 Ag-FDA package provides $7.1 billion for FDA and $1.28 billion for the Human Foods Program, with riders restricting new population-wide sodium actions, limiting new actions on low-risk ready-to-eat foods for Listeria, and delaying the traceability rule to July 20, 2028. FDA's budget request carried 27 legislative proposals, five touching food: expanded contaminant authority, mandatory post-market chemical data submissions, destruction authority for high-risk imports, biennial foreign facility registration fees, and authority to share food-safety information with state, local, tribal, and territorial authorities.

## Takeaways

Closing themes are to expect major policy changes announced outside formal rulemaking, continued leadership instability that has so far spared the Human Foods Program, a growing tension between MAHA priorities and midterm affordability pressures, and an agency motivated to mark visible wins on dye phaseout, infant formula, GRAS reform, and post-market chemical reviews heading into the election. For labelers, the live watch items are the FOP redesign direction, the UPF definition, the GRAS rule scope, and the SNAP restriction litigation.
