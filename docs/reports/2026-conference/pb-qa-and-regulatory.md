# pb-qa-and-regulatory

Effective QA and Regulatory collaboration rests on distinct roles, seven concrete teamwork skills, and a repeatable five-step decision framework for resolving compliance pressure.

## Summary

Belinda Davis, Director of FSQA Labeling and Regulatory Compliance at Tyson Foods, presented a practitioner's playbook for QA and Regulatory teamwork in a USDA-FSIS and FDA regulated manufacturing environment. The session defines each function's lane, names seven collaboration skills, supplies a five-step decision spine, and works three real-world case scenarios.

## Key Points

Davis separated the two roles cleanly: Regulatory owns what the product must legally say and claim to be compliant, covering laws, enforcement risk, labeling, claims, approvals, and defensibility, while Quality Assurance owns what the process must consistently do to make that product safely, covering food-safety systems, supplier and process verification, execution, validation, and monitoring. They overlap on specifications, ingredients and allergens, change management, and consumer protection, captured in the line that Regulatory defines what must be true on paper while QA ensures it is true every time the product runs.

The seven collaboration skills are a shared risk mindset that asks first what the consumer risk is if something fails, early and continuous engagement from ideation through launch, clear role clarity that separates interpretation from verification, communication and translation between regulatory language and process reality, specification discipline built on a single agreed source of truth with no undocumented changes, mutual respect that treats both functions as strategic rather than as gatekeeper or mere execution, and audit-ready documentation that is documented, reproducible, and defensible months or years later. The decision framework is a five-step spine: identify the trigger, assess the full risk across consumer safety, regulatory exposure, and business continuity, map decision rights between QA and Regulatory at each stage, establish the controls and proof that the process worked, and lock in changes to prevent recurrence. The three case studies, excess sauce on a Child Nutrition item, an incoming ingredient label that does not match the approved specification while production is scheduled and the plant asks whether it can run, and a marketing-driven clean-label reformulation, each exercise the spine and the question of where QA's authority ends and Regulatory's begins.

## Takeaways

QA and Regulatory have distinct roles but a shared mission of protecting the consumer and the brand, collaboration is a practicable skill set rather than just a mindset, the five-step spine turns acute compliance pressure into a repeatable process, and early collaboration prevents costly surprises on spec changes, clean-label initiatives, and line-side ingredient issues. The orientation is meat-and-poultry and FSIS-leaning, so the Child Nutrition and "can we run" scenarios are most directly applicable to USDA-regulated operations.
