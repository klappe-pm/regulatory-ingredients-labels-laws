# gensess-fda-and-food-policy-update

FDA's 2026 food labeling agenda spans nutrition, food chemical safety, and microbiological safety, with the headline moves on ultra-processed foods, front-of-pack labeling, the "healthy" claim, and closing the GRAS loophole.

## Summary

Laura Carroll, Senior Advisor for Nutrition Outreach in FDA's Human Foods Program, gave the agency's own update on labeling policy priorities. She framed FDA's role as empowering consumers to identify healthier choices and helping create a healthier food supply, noting FDA regulates about 80 percent of the food supply, then walked through the 2026 Human Foods Program priority deliverables organized around nutrition, food chemical safety, and microbiological food safety.

## Key Points

On nutrition, the update referenced the 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines for Americans and positioned ultra-processed foods as a key MAHA-aligned priority, with FDA and USDA working toward a uniform UPF definition. The front-of-package nutrition labeling effort would add a "Nutrition Info" box rating saturated fat, sodium, and added sugars as Low, Medium, or High on the principal display panel. Additional nutrition priorities include voluntary caffeine labeling guidance, an added-sugars reduction strategy, implementation of the updated "healthy" nutrient-content claim, and sodium reduction. The agency is also continuing analysis of its portfolio of more than 250 standards of identity, having announced in July 2025 that it is revoking or proposing to revoke 52 of them, and proposing to modernize the decades-old standard for pasteurized orange juice.

On allergens, Carroll flagged a February 2026 virtual public meeting on food allergen thresholds and a January 2026 request for information on labeling and preventing cross-contact of gluten in packaged foods. On food chemical safety, FDA has initiated the process to revoke authorization of two petroleum-based dyes, approved new colors from natural sources, launched a public tracker of industry commitments, is providing enforcement discretion for voluntary "no artificial colors" claims, and has developed a proposed rule to close the GRAS loophole. The agency also finalized a science-based post-market chemical assessment program and launched reassessments of butylated hydroxytoluene and azodicarbonamide.

## Takeaways

This is the agency's authoritative read on its own 2026 priorities, useful as the federal baseline against which the state-level and litigation sessions should be cross-checked. The most actionable threads for labelers are the converging "healthy" claim and front-of-pack timelines, the synthetic-dye phase-out paired with natural-color approvals and "no artificial colors" enforcement discretion, and the GRAS loophole proposed rule.
