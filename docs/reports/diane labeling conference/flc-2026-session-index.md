## Cross-Cutting Threads

Several themes recur across the nine sessions and are worth tracking as a set rather than per deck. The "healthy" nutrient-content claim transition appears in the FDA update, the MAHA briefing, the claims breakout, and the regulatory-landscape playbook, with a consistent message that the fortification rescue is closing and a possible further revision to align with the new Dietary Guidelines. Front-of-package nutrition labeling runs through the same decks, with the open question being whether FDA departs from the proposed monochromatic box toward a traffic-light scheme and whether it re-proposes or finalizes.

GRAS reform is a second spine, framed federally as closing the loophole through forward-looking or retrospective reporting and at the state level as GRAS-disclosure databases such as New York S1239F. The ultra-processed-food definition is a third, unresolved federally while California and other states advance their own definitions and certification seals. The synthetic-dye phaseout and natural-color approvals connect the FDA update, the MAHA briefing, and both state decks, paired with enforcement discretion for voluntary "no artificial colors" claims.

The strongest organizing idea across the legal sessions is that the compliance problem is now federal and state at once. The claims breakout supplies the discipline, that the documented dossier and claims register are the product and that regulatory belongs at the concept stage, while the regulatory-landscape breakout supplies the operating playbook, one master inventory feeding reformulate, relabel, restrict, or defend decisions. California compliance and Prop 65 sit alongside as a state-specific liability regime that most national brands cannot avoid.

## Provenance and Limits

These notes are intake captures of the slide decks, not verified legal or regulatory advice. Specific statutory citations, case names, bill numbers, dollar figures, and dates are recorded as the speakers presented them and are flagged in each note as unverified. The Law Talk deck is title-level only, so its substance depends on Diane's notes or a recording. Several of the MAHA and regulatory-landscape items are fast-moving and should be confirmed against fda.gov or primary state sources before any external use.
