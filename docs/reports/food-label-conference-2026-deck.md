# Five forces are rewriting the label

1. **GRAS** — the self-affirmed loophole is closing
2. **Dyes** — synthetic colors phasing out
3. **UPF** — a definition is coming
4. **Front-of-package** — an interpretive nutrition box
5. **"Healthy" & claims** — food-group model, net impression

<!-- Preview the journey without spoiling. Each gets its own slide next. -->

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# Force 1 — GRAS

## The "loophole" is closing

FDA proposed rule to **end self-affirmed GRAS** (OIRA review since Dec 1).
Post-market reassessment of **BHA, BHT, ADA**.
States pile on: **NY S1239F** GRAS-disclosure database.

<!-- Self-GRAS has let companies self-certify ingredients. That pathway is narrowing federally AND states are building disclosure registries. Inventory every self-GRAS ingredient now. -->

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# Force 2 — Dyes

## Synthetic colors are on the way out

6 petroleum dyes: voluntary phase-out by **end of 2026**. Red No. 3 accelerated.
"No artificial color" claims now under **enforcement discretion**.
Natural swaps (beetroot, spirulina) bring **their own** label questions.

<!-- Federal is mostly voluntary + "understanding," not formal rule. The teeth are at the state level — see CA AB 418 / AB 2316 and TX SB 25. -->

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# Force 3 — Ultra-processed foods

## A definition is coming — just not agreed

FDA/USDA joint RFI closed Oct 2025: **5,000-19,000 comments**, no consensus.
**No federal timeline.** States are not waiting.
California is already drafting its own definition (AB 1264, AB 2244).

<!-- The risk: divergent state definitions before a federal one exists. Watch whether NOVA or nutrient criteria win. -->

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# Force 4 — Front-of-package

## An interpretive box on the front panel

FDA "Nutrition Info" box rates three nutrients **Low / Med / High**:
**Saturated Fat · Sodium · Added Sugars**
Final rule projected **spring 2026** · compliance **3-4 years**

<!-- Docket FDA-2024-N-2910. Kennedy has floated a green/yellow/red traffic light, which could mean a new NPRM — don't lock packaging to the Biden-era design. -->

<!-- _class: lead -->

# Force 5 — "Healthy" & claims

## Fortification no longer rescues a claim

New **food-group model** (effective Apr 2025, compliance **Feb 25, 2028**).
Gains the claim: avocados, nuts, salmon, olive oil.
Loses it: fortified white bread, sweetened cereal.

<!-- The "fortification trap." Audit the healthy-claimed portfolio before the 2028 deadline and align it with the FOP relabel into ONE redesign window. -->

# California writes the national template

| Law | What it does | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **AB 418** | Bans BVO, bromate, propylparaben, Red 3 | Jan 1, 2027 |
| **AB 2316** | 6 school dyes out | Dec 31, 2027 |
| **AB 660** | Standard date labels; kills "sell by" | Jul 1, 2026 |
| **AB 1264** | UPF definition + school phase-out | 2028 / 2035 |
| **AB 2034** | CDPH premarket notice | Jul 1, 2027 |

<!-- If you comply with California, you are most of the way to compliant everywhere. That is the planning shortcut. -->

# The patchwork beyond California

**Texas SB 25** — 44-ingredient warnings, Jan 2027 *(enjoined)*
**New York S1239F** — GRAS database + dye bans *(awaiting Governor)*
School-food laws enacted: **Iowa · Nebraska · Tennessee**

Litigation delays laws. It does not reverse the scrutiny.

# How states get around federal law

Federal law (NLEA 403A) preempts most state label rules...

## ...but **safety warnings are not preempted.**

That single carve-out is the lane every state bill drives through.

<!-- This is the legal mechanic that explains the whole patchwork. Warning labels and disclosure mandates survive preemption; nutrition-content rules do not. -->

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# Proposition 65 is its own engine

Food notices jumped **~360 → 1,200+ per year** and stayed high.

The **defendant** must prove exposure is below the safe harbor.
Many chemicals have **no** safe harbor. Fees-to-penalty ratio: **~20x**.

<!-- Highest risk: heavy metals (lead, cadmium, arsenic) in roots, fish, rice, spices; acrylamide in cooked carbs. Build the defense file BEFORE the 60-day notice arrives. -->

# On claims: the dossier **is** the product

If it isn't documented, the claim isn't defensible.

Courts judge the **net impression** — words, images, color, icons.
No settled definition? **The plaintiffs' bar sets the standard.**

<!-- "Natural," "clean," "guilt-free," "GLP-1 friendly" have no federal definition. Default to specific, measurable nutrient claims. Every spec-sheet change — and every influencer post — is a claims-register event. -->

---

# What this means for beverages

- **Added sugar** — the likeliest FOP "High" trigger
- **Sweeteners** — allulose, stevia, sucralose are next
- **Caffeine** — voluntary federal guidance + Canada thresholds
- **Color & preservative swaps** — route back through claim review

<!-- Sugar is the most politically charged nutrient of 2026. The DGA line — "no amount of added sugars... is recommended" — will be quoted in litigation. -->

# Make QA + Regulatory one motion

Regulatory: what must be **true on paper**.
QA: what must be true **every run**.

**Trigger → Risk → Decision Rights → Controls & Proof → Prevent Recurrence**

# From patchwork to playbook

**Was:** many trackers · reactive firefighting · "federal *or* state"

**Now:** one inventory · change-control gates · "both, at once"

<!-- The Sparkline turn: current reality vs. the better future. This is where the audience becomes the hero. -->

# The eight-step playbook

1. One **master** ingredient & claims inventory
2. Classify each ingredient's regulatory status
3. Map triggers by **function, not name**
4. Install a label-change **control gate**
5. Keep GRAS & post-market **dossiers audit-ready**
6. Segment: **Reformulate / Relabel / Restrict / Defend**
7. Update supplier **contracts & attestations**
8. Align legal, regulatory, science & comms on **one calendar**
