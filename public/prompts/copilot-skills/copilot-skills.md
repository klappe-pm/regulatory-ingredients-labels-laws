# copilot-skills

Plain-language guides for using Microsoft 365 Copilot inside the apps a regulatory labeling specialist lives in: Copilot Chat, Excel, Outlook, Word, and SharePoint. Each guide states what Copilot can and cannot do in that app, a one-time setup, a few copy-ready recipes for real labeling tasks, and the guardrails that keep Copilot assistive rather than authoritative. These are skills in the practical sense: repeatable ways to get Copilot to help in one surface, written for a first-time user.

Start with [[copilot-chat-basics]]; it teaches the moves (attaching files, the `/` reference, what a good prompt looks like) that every other guide assumes. Then read the guide for whichever app you are in.

## skills

- [[copilot-chat-basics]]: the foundation. Attaching files, referencing with `/`, what Copilot can and cannot do, and how to read its answers critically.
- [[copilot-in-excel]]: formula and ingredient tables, Nutrition Facts math scaffolding, data cleanup, and why you still check every number.
- [[copilot-in-outlook]]: drafting and replying in your voice, summarizing threads, and pulling action items, without auto-sending.
- [[copilot-in-word]]: drafting and editing label and memo documents, comparing versions, and formatting, with change tracking.
- [[copilot-in-sharepoint]]: finding the current label of record, grounding Copilot on a document library, and the limits of site search.

## how-these-relate-to-the-prompts

The prompt categories ([[claims-compliance]], [[content-derivation]], and the rest) are the what: specific compliance tasks with exact wording. These skill guides are the how: getting Copilot to help you run those prompts inside the app where your files already are. Where a prompt says "attach the formula", the app guide tells you exactly how in Excel or SharePoint.

## workflow

1. Read [[copilot-chat-basics]] once.
2. When a prompt tells you to attach an input, use the matching app guide to do it.
3. When you find yourself repeating an app task, run [[skill-gap-finder]] to decide whether it deserves a saved prompt or its own recipe here.
