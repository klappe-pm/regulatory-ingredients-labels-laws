## box-detail

### box-1-objective

Set up a centralized, cross-functional Federal and State Regulatory Tracker by the close of KOE season on 2026-11-02, that proactively monitors, analyzes, and communicates food and beverage compliance changes, and reduces regulatory risk.

Restated as an outcome rather than an artifact: by November 2026, no regulatory change material to a Talking Rain label reaches a deadline without a named owner having seen it, in writing, with its source.

### box-2-business-rationale

Federal action and a fast-moving patchwork of state laws now land on the same can at the same time. The objective is not to predict which rule wins, it is to stay organized enough to move quickly when any of them lands. Today the signal arrives as scattered email and ad hoc forwards, which is not a control anyone can audit. One sourced list, read the same way by leadership, brand, R&D, and legal, converts regulatory change from a surprise into a scheduled decision.

### box-3-scope

In scope: US FDA food-safety and labeling changes that directly affect beverages, state-level ingredient and labeling law, and what can be gleaned from Canada and Mexico as the other markets sold into. Every entry carries a plain summary, the impact, what it means for the beverage business, and a link to the original source.

Out of scope: legal advice, label approval, and any automated edit to the register or change log. Detection and ingestion flag for human review only. Anything touching a live label is confirmed against the source and counsel before action.

### box-4-deliverables

- A centralized register of tracked regulations with source links and effective dates.
- A change log recording what moved, when it was detected, and who reviewed it.
- Briefing output at three depths: v1 headlines for everyone, v2 context and citation for leads, v3 full detail with action and affected products for the response owner.
- A routing map naming the accountable function for each change type across R&D, brand, legal, and leadership.
- A published cadence and depth decision: which audience receives v1, v2, or v3, and on what schedule. This is the owner's call to make and close, not an open question carried through the season.
- A runbook that lets a second person operate the tracker without the original owner.

### box-5-milestones

| Window | Milestone | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|---|
| August 2026 | Baseline register populated and the current federal and state watchlist locked. | Register covers the known 2027 California dates, dyes, BVO, and Red No. 3. |
| September 2026 | Cross-functional review with R&D, brand, legal, and leadership; cadence and depth decided and published. | Named reviewer per function and a written cadence decision. |
| October 2026 | First full pilot cycle published end to end and revised on reviewer feedback. | One briefing shipped at all three depths, feedback logged and applied. |
| November 2026 | Second cycle shipped on cadence, runbook handed over, KOE closed. | Sign-off recorded on or before 2026-11-02. |

### box-6-success-measures

- Cadence adherence: briefings published on the agreed schedule, target 100 percent of committed cycles in the pilot window.
- Traceability: every register entry carries a working link to the primary source, target 100 percent.
- Coverage: zero material effective dates missed during the measurement window.
- Lead time: reformulation-relevant changes reach R&D and suppliers with usable runway rather than after the fact.
- Accountability: every open item has a named owner and a next action, target 100 percent.
- Adoption: each reviewing function (R&D, brand, legal, leadership) confirms the depth they receive is the depth they need.

### box-7-dependencies

- A named reviewer accountable in R&D, brand, legal, and leadership, since a cross-functional tracker with one reader is a personal spreadsheet. Securing those names is the first true external dependency, because the goal owner can decide the cadence but cannot appoint another function's reviewer.
- A durable publication surface the team already reads, so the briefing is not another inbox.
- Continued access to primary sources, and time in the calendar for the review cycle during launch periods.

### box-8-risks

| Risk | Effect if it lands | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Source drift, a monitored page moves or changes shape. | Silent gap in coverage that looks like quiet. | Detection failures surface as review flags, never as an empty result treated as no news. |
| False confidence in an unverified citation. | A label decision made on a stale or misread rule. | Citations stay marked unverified until confirmed against the current source; nothing here is legal advice. |
| Single-owner concentration. | The tracker stops when one person is out. | Runbook and handover are deliverables, not follow-up work. |
| Cadence slips under launch load. | The tracker becomes shelfware before it proves value. | Commit to the smallest cadence that is genuinely sustainable, then raise it. |
| Scope creep into full multi-market coverage. | November date missed. | US primary, Canada and Mexico observational only, in this KOE window. |

### box-9-definition-of-done

The goal is done when two consecutive briefing cycles have shipped on the agreed cadence, each reviewing function (R&D, brand, legal, leadership) has a named owner who has signed off that the depth they receive is usable, the register and change log are current with sourced entries and no unreviewed detection flags, and the runbook has been handed to a second operator who has run one cycle from it. Sign-off recorded on or before 2026-11-02.
